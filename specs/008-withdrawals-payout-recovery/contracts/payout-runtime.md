# P08 Proposed Protected Payout and Recovery Contract

This design applies only to Group B after reservation/scheduling Gate A passes. It extends existing protected custody/financial runtime controls; it does not expose an HTTP signing endpoint or authorize a transfer. See the [spec](../spec.md), [research](../research.md), [data model](../data-model.md) and [public contract](withdrawals.md).

## Ownership and authority

Proposed payout intent, attempt, reconciliation, settlement and recovery responsibilities belong in `apps/api/src/modules/withdrawals/`. The existing `apps/api/src/worker.ts` lifecycle adds bounded withdrawal wakeups/scans; the existing `apps/api/src/signer.ts` lifecycle composes protected payout work. Public app/router imports must neither load treasury keys nor start private workers. Current sweep-owned `TreasurySweep`, `TransferAttempt` and `modules/treasury/` workflow remain sweep-owned. Share concrete lower-level TRC-20 construction, validation/signing and protected storage primitives where genuinely common; do not introduce a generic transfer engine.

Use the existing `FinancialRuntimeAdmission`, shared control fence/generation, independently acknowledged fresh process boots, emergency new-dispatch pause and `p06_recovery_operator` recovery boundary. Extend actual SQL grants/guards for new tables/columns rather than trusting broad integration-fixture grants. API may accept/read requests within its admitted financial role; the scheduling worker may discover/lease wakeup work without keys or private attempt authority; signer may sign/broadcast only admitted fixed intents; the independent recovery operator admits recovered authority. Ordinary API, worker and signer cannot approve their own boot/recovery generation or unfence a restored database.

The signer receives a strict authenticated durable intent, not employee/admin/provider-submitted signing fields. Its allowlist is operation `WITHDRAWAL_PAYOUT`, request ID, attempt ID, employee owner ID, accepted reservation/business identity, configured network/token contract, immutable treasury-source key binding, original recipient/version, exact accepted net micro-units, accepted intent digest and independently enforced payout/resource policy snapshot. It rereads authoritative request/allocation/destination-version history, verifies each binding and current dispatch controls, and rejects forged/mismatched IDs, arbitrary calls, key export and unknown operations. Later destination replacement cannot rewrite this intent. Employee expiry/upgrade cannot reinterpret an accepted eligible request.

## Treasury key and independent execution policy

The treasury payout key is a new protected provisioning responsibility; it is not an employee deposit assignment. A proposed owner-only provisioning input derives the public treasury address from protected key material, matches configured network/source, writes immutable encrypted key identity, obtains independent off-host recovery PUT/GET digest acknowledgement and then binds public identity/acknowledgement in PostgreSQL. Missing acknowledgement or conflicting source/configuration fails closed. No credential or plaintext key is accepted in public API, HTTP job payloads, command arguments, logs, browser bundles or repo files.

Reuse existing owner-only protected files, private paths outside the project/proofs, no-symlink/containment checks, authenticated AES-GCM envelope context and separately protected encryption/recovery credentials. The signer entrypoint alone loads its credentials. The recovery service's role remains independently scoped; same-host encryption/process separation reduce risk but do not protect against a fully compromised host.

Add explicit envelope types `TREASURY_KEY`, `PAYOUT_SIGNED_ATTEMPT` and `PAYOUT_BROADCAST_INTENT` to the existing `apps/api/src/infrastructure/custody/encrypted-envelope.ts`, `recovery-store.protocol.ts` and their concrete type allowlists/dispatch owners. Preserve existing envelope format/protocol and authenticated binding of type/object ID/version/key ID. Existing sweep recovery parses current `SIGNED_ATTEMPT` and `BROADCAST_INTENT` as sweeps; payout records must never enter that parser. LIST is bounded and type-specific; PUT/GET acknowledgements/readback must match exact type, object/version and digest. Private records bind request/attempt/intent, source key, unsigned body/TxID and signed/broadcast identity so a wrong record cannot restore authority.

Independent signer policy bounds maximum payout net units, energy fee limit and total company resource cost. These are security/resource ceilings, not a second business withdrawal limit or fee change. No cap may silently alter the accepted net; a violated cap leaves durable blocked work and an operator-visible reason. Independently verify configured network/token/source, latest source account permissions, TRX/resources, recipient-specific exact TRC-20 simulation and token liquidity before signing and before a same-byte rebroadcast. Sample token balance at a coherent solidified block at or after the last completed lane's final block; stale samples cannot reuse already spent treasury funds. A provider timeout/malformed response is a blocker, not evidence of sufficient resources or safe failure.

Proposed typed settings below belong to `core/config/withdrawal.config.ts`, except the email target in existing email configuration and protected key/caps in existing custody/TRON configuration. They are design choices, not installed environment keys. No configuration is exposed to the browser or logged as a credential.

Configure independent protected maximum payout units and company fee/resource ceilings explicitly alongside the acknowledged treasury key record identity; missing/invalid protected settings fail closed. A conservative initial payout-unit ceiling is 500000000 micro-USDT (500 USDT), sufficient for initial net amounts and independent of employee-submitted authority. Future business bounds do not silently raise the signer cap. Reuse existing bounded provider timeout/private-file controls. Redis producers use bounded connect/retry and no offline queue; consumers use `maxRetriesPerRequest: null`. Job IDs contain only `withdrawal-<uuid>-<scheduleVersion>` with no colon. Bound retained jobs/attempts, handle error events, and stop discovery then close Worker, Queue, owned Redis connections and DB on shutdown. Reconciliation/new signer work is independent of Redis availability.

Maintain one unresolved outgoing lane per configured network/token/treasury source through a PostgreSQL partial unique guard. It serializes potentially live payouts across workers/restarts so two workers cannot independently spend one treasury sample. Other accepted requests remain durably scheduled. The lane closes only on verified final settlement or conclusive safe failure; UNKNOWN blocks subsequent outgoing treasury sends while its reconciliation, reads, deposits and inbound sweeps continue. Inbound sweeps add funds and keep their existing ownership; no automatic top-up engine, admin payout approval or employee resource deduction is introduced.

## Queue discovery handoff

SCHEDULED acceptance/extension starts with `nextCheckAt = NULL`. A due current-schedule-version wakeup may set only that null hint to server time, under worker mutation admission and only while no attempt exists. Periodic worker scans repair missed wakeups. Signer selects due hinted rows in its fast bounded existing loop, and periodically scans due unhinted rows independently (initial `WITHDRAWAL_REPAIR_INTERVAL_MS = 30000`, integer 1000–300000 and no shorter than configured scan interval). No Redis credential/client is loaded in signer. Future retry backoff is never shortened by queue delivery; extension resets the hint with the new schedule revision. Paused/fenced/restricted hints grant no dispatch rights. Protected signer claim is the sole financial send-authority boundary. Tests cover real Redis hint delivery, duplicate/stale hints, queue/worker loss, authoritative fallback and preserved backoff.

## Durable ordering and replay

1. A Redis job contains only request ID and schedule version. A bounded PostgreSQL scan repairs missing wakeups. Both reread state/deadline/version, financial mutation admission and weekday rules. Candidate scans do not retain request locks before the shared financial lock order. Duplicate/stale jobs are no-ops. Scheduler discovery leases cannot create a private attempt, take a treasury lane or move a request to SIGNING.
2. The protected signer alone uses current `assertDispatchAdmission` and the shared financial transaction boundary to move a due safely scheduled request to SIGNING and take its treasury lane. The claim fixes one unique attempt, immutable accepted intent and original recipient. Ban/block/cancellation/extension uses the same ordered locks: unsent action wins with exact release, or protected claim wins with no refund. Capture server time after locks; no new send precedes the counted deadline/normalized weekday dispatch. Group A tests the conditional safe-unsent/claim boundary using an admitted protected test identity without any signing/broadcast; no Group B external execution begins early.
3. Validate and durably retain one unsigned object and transaction identity/TxID in protected preparation storage before local signing. Concurrent preparation must reuse the committed winner. A lost signing reply recovers the same attempt and body, never rebuilds a new reference block/expiry/TxID. Signing recovery may finish that identical object or recover its existing signed archive winner; it must not create an untracked independent payment. If a committed original identity survives but original body bytes are lost before signed archive acknowledgement, a matching recovered signed record may supply that same body. Otherwise retain UNKNOWN, reservation and source lane with RECOVERY_UNAVAILABLE; missing inventory keeps recovery fenced. Never reconstruct a fresh body or TxID to fill the gap.
4. Before any possible broadcast, retain immutable signed bytes locally, PUT the exact encrypted `PAYOUT_SIGNED_ATTEMPT`, verify acknowledgement/readback and commit the same signed identity in PostgreSQL. If DB/local/off-host evidence disagrees or an acknowledgement is missing, sending stops and protected recovery diagnoses the gap. An unsigned or signed object alone is not a settled payout.
5. Commit admitted durable broadcast intent and potentially live UNKNOWN state before network I/O; obtain matching recoverable `PAYOUT_BROADCAST_INTENT` acknowledgement before invoking broadcast. Do not hold a PostgreSQL transaction across provider, Redis, email or archive I/O. Pause/admission checks precede this irreversible boundary. A pause arriving afterward cannot claim that the already admitted transfer was never sent.
6. Broadcast acknowledgements may move observation state to SUBMITTED, but timeout/lost reply/crash leaves active reserved uncertainty. Reconciliation precedes any retry; when still valid and independently admitted, retry uses the exact retained signed bytes/TxID. Queue retry count, absence from one response, local expiry or claim age never authorizes a new body, new attempt, refund or replacement payment.

SIGNED/SUBMITTED/UNKNOWN are recoverable observation states of the same immutable attempt. Leases can transfer responsibility to recover/reconcile that attempt only. New-dispatch pause and weekends prevent new sends, including rebroadcast admission, while canonical observation continues every day. Restore fencing additionally prevents unadmitted financial settlement/release; observation must still establish the evidence needed for independent recovery admission.

## Canonical observation and financial completion

Provider data is unknown input parsed at the existing infrastructure boundary. Evidence must match the original unsigned/signed body and TxID, configured network/token, treasury source, immutable recipient/version and exact net, with solidified canonical block membership and full execution receipt. A broadcast success, explorer URL, receipt alone without canonical linkage, confirmation count guess, malformed/nonfinal/disappearing response or a transaction on another network cannot settle.

Successful execution requires exact successful TRC-20 transfer evidence with no conflicting execution disposition. The shared financial transaction consumes the original gross allocation once: available deltas zero, reserved deltas negative exact original NON_REFERRAL/REFERRAL allocation, allocation ACTIVE→SETTLED, one append-only `SETTLE` operation with immutable accepted net plus fee equalling gross and required audit. The same transaction sets the request to `COMPLETED`, the original attempt to `CONFIRMED_SUCCESS`, closes the source lane and monotonically advances its durable final-block floor. This is consumption of reserved ownership, not another debit. Failure of any dependent write leaves the whole completion uncommitted; duplicate/concurrent observations resolve to one saved settlement.

Initial automatic safe-failure evidence is independently verified canonical-final `CHAIN_FAILED` for the original call/body/TxID, failed execution and no conflicting transfer logs/live attempt. That evidence closes FAILED and atomically releases the exact original gross/source allocation once, with no platform fee; any incurred network cost remains a company cost. The lane closes in the same terminal boundary. A later payment is a new withdrawal under current eligibility/limits/terms with a new 72-counted-hour schedule, without a 24-hour cooldown. P08 has no replacement-payment operation.

The clarified specification permits another conclusive non-executability proof only if a dedicated validated verifier establishes complete coverage. The current provider does not establish that verifier; expiration, empty RPC results and a provider outage therefore remain UNKNOWN. Missing, mismatched or incomplete evidence retains the active request, original reservation and lane, with bounded protected escalation. Never turn incomplete observations into a guessed success, refund or repayment.

## Recovery inventory and operator outcomes

Extend existing `modules/custody/recovery-history.ts`, `runtime-control.ts`, `recovery.cli.ts` and payout-owned recovery readers to include destination/version and current pending-proof authority, append-only `WithdrawalDestinationAudit` identities/rows, quote acceptance/business identity, policy facts, request/deadlines/extensions/actions, exact reservation/release/settlement history, treasury key binding/acknowledgement, unsigned/signed attempt identity, broadcast intents and canonical terminal evidence. Hash singleton policy facts separately from existing UUID-table inventory logic. Reconciliation must detect missing post-snapshot destination events, accepted requests/actions/history as well as chain-visible sends; chain scans cannot reconstruct off-chain terms or authorization.

The proposed Group A `WithdrawalDestinationAudit` belongs to withdrawals and exists independently of any request. Only successful committed issuance/resend (`PROOF_ISSUED`) and consumption (`PROOF_CONSUMED`) append events, in the same transaction as proof supersession or first-address save/pending-authority clear. Rejected commands append no event; replay/races cannot duplicate a committed event. Rows contain no token, proof hash or full URL. Recovery must verify these event identities/rows against restored destination/proof authority, including post-snapshot events before the first request; missing/conflicting audit history retains the fence. This adds no API or generic audit framework.

A fresh boot/restore stays financially fenced and new dispatch paused until the independent operator verifies recovered post-snapshot commits, complete custody/request/attempt/history inventory, original in-flight outcomes and reconciled cutoff. Ordinary runtimes cannot assert their own evidence. Missing/conflicting history, unreachable archives, wrong key/network, unknown potentially live attempts or stale source-floor evidence keep the fence/recovery gap visible. Resume does not skip weekday/deadline/policy checks or rewrite saved requests. P08 proves isolated withdrawal recovery; deployed WAL/PITR/full-system release proof remains P11.

Reuse bounded `RuntimeSignals` events/allowlisted metadata for pending work, resource/liquidity shortfall, unresolved attempt, recovery unavailable, evidence conflict and admission. Proposed request/source identifiers must be explicitly allowlisted; keys, credentials, signed/unsigned payloads, email links, private paths and raw provider errors never enter signals. Keep bounded ACTIVE/REMINDER/CLEARED behavior and verify actual operational alert sink/configuration during implementation acceptance. Emergency pause/resume reuses existing protected control with authorized actor/time/nonblank reason audit; it adds no public admin approval step.

Future tests must exercise real PostgreSQL/Redis claims and loss, protected Linux file/archive/protocol authority, crash points around preparation/signing/archive/intent/broadcast, exact-byte replay, canonical wrong/pending/failed/success evidence, one lane, atomic settlement/release, independent boot/restore admission and separately opted-in payout testnet evidence. Concrete target files/commands and ordered gates are in the [quickstart](../quickstart.md). The one-entry ledger-label exception was explicitly approved by the owner on 2026-10-08; implement and verify only that approved change alongside Group B shared contracts, as recorded in the [public contract](withdrawals.md#observable-errors-and-compatibility-gate). No implementation tests or live transactions ran during PLAN.
